Method

How an engagement runs

1

Explain the Name

Ten minutes, every single kickoff, without exception. There is a slide. The slide has a photograph of Gerald's grandfather on it.

2

Scope

We map what you are genuinely exposed to, rather than what your last consultant found easiest to draw. The list is usually shorter and considerably worse.

3

Test

Controls are sampled, not admired. We pull the files, follow the money that was supposed to move, and note every place the process only works when someone remembers.

4

Report

A findings document written to be read by a regulator — because sooner or later, one of them reads it. No adjectives that cannot be evidenced.

Selected Matters

Cases, anonymised to the point of rudeness

Compliance

Regional Bank, 400,000 Alerts, Nobody Reading Them

An alerting system tuned so conservatively that the compliance team had quietly stopped opening it eighteen months earlier.

Outcome: False positives down 71%. The eleven alerts that actually mattered were escalated within a fortnight.

The bank asked us not to name them. They have never asked us to change our name, which we appreciate.

Forensics

Family Business, Two Sets of Books, One Nervous Nephew

The founder wanted the nephew investigated. We asked to see nine years of ledgers first, which is not what he had in mind.

Outcome: Discrepancy traced to a single long-serving employee. Matter referred. Recovery ongoing.

The nephew was innocent throughout and remains, we are told, nervous.

Compliance

Payments Startup That Grew Faster Than Its Onboarding

Eleven months of extraordinary growth, built on a client-verification process designed for roughly four hundred users.

Outcome: 180,000 client files re-verified in eleven weeks. Licence retained.

"Retained" is carrying a great deal of weight in that sentence, but it is accurate.

Garments

Hotel Group, 1,200 Covers A Night, One Linen Contract

Their previous supplier lost roughly a tablecloth an hour and invoiced for it monthly, in arrears, without comment.

Outcome: Turnaround from 48 hours to 14. Linen loss down 60%. Contract renewed twice.

This is the case the partners bring up at dinner. It is never the one clients ask about.

FAQ

The four questions, in order of frequency

Is the name a joke?

The name is 1978 — two surnames on a brass plate, in the order the partners settled over one dinner. The joke arrived later, at no cost to us, and has never once left.

Do you launder money?

We launder linen, wool and the occasional silk. Anything else that comes through the door is logged, declined, and where the rules require it, reported. This is the shortest answer we give and by some margin the one we give most.

Why has the firm never rebranded?

It was costed in 2011 at £42,000. The partners voted to buy a new press instead. The press is still running and the phone is still ringing, so the vote is generally held to have been sound.

Are you connected to the other Four Schmucks companies?

We audit them. It goes badly for them every time, and they keep booking us anyway, which our forensics team finds more interesting than the audits.
